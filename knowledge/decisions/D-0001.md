# D-0001 — Physical atlas and location-provider protocol

Accepted 2026-09-20.

Rusty requested player head icons, navigation while moving, owned-village and
personal C.A.M.P. markers with operator teleport, and personal landmarks. They
requested a Minecraft-style UI and a 6.031-style protocol for new location types.
They explicitly selected reveal-through-exploration, vanilla maps and cartography
as the substrate, and then selected an atlas over separate enhanced map sheets.

The accepted proposal joins real vanilla map sheets into a continuous view,
preserves shared map IDs, leaves missing terrain blank, supports an offhand travel
view and a full interactive view, and separates terrain from live locations.
Locked terrain remains frozen; live markers are independent of that lock.

Implementation defaults: a map plus a book creates the atlas at a cartography
table; atlas plus map binds a sheet; atlas plus shears recovers the last sheet.
These inexpensive recipes are implementation choices, not separate quotations
of Rusty's approval. Up to 64 distinct map IDs are carried in vanilla container
components; maps can be recovered for vanilla processing. Coarse sheets draw
behind finer sheets. Bookmarks are private world-saved player data. Player and
village locations are public; C.A.M.P. locations are private to their owners.

The public provider port is JDK-only with immutable snapshots, stable namespaced
identities, explicit visibility and re-resolution at action time. NeoForge wiring,
storage, transport and rendering are adapters. Teleport always checks current
operator permission and destination safety on the server. Queries do not load
distant chunks; an explicit teleport may load an existing destination chunk.

This decision authorizes implementation, not publication or deployment.
